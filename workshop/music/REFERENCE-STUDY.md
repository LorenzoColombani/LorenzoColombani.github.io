# After Hours — reference study and composition notes

Reference: The Clash, “Should I Stay or Should I Go”. This is an analysis-led,
original instrumental for The Workshop; no source recording or lyric is used.

## Evidence consulted

- [Huw Foster's bass transcription](https://www.huwfoster.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Should-I-Stay-or-Should-I-Go-notation.pdf):
  4/4, tempo marked 112, regular-time sections alternating with a double-time feel.
  The verse's bass rests are part of its phrasing, not missing accompaniment.
- [SpyTunes guitar lesson and harmonic analysis](https://spytunes.com/should-i-stay-or-should-i-go-chords/):
  D-centred blues-derived harmony, a flattened-third chord colour, clipped
  verse statements, contrasting guitar voicings and a busier chorus.
- [JustinGuitar lesson](https://www.justinguitar.com/songs/the-clash-should-i-stay-or-should-i-go-chords-tabs-guitar-lesson-sg-113):
  chord embellishments and strumming are distinct parts of the arrangement.
- [Official recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BN1WwnEDWAM), linked for
  listening comparison. No claim of an uninterrupted aural review by the agent.

## Musical decisions for our cue

Tempo 114 BPM; 40 bars (84.211 seconds); 4/4. Our own E-centred eight-bar
phrases and melodic replies, rather than reproducing the song's riff/form.

1. **Space and answer:** short strummed power-chord statements leave gaps for a
   higher guitar response. Vary note lengths as well as velocities.
2. **Change perceived speed without racing the clock:** the lift keeps the same
   tempo, placing snares on the offbeats for a double-time feel.
3. **One rhythm section:** kick and bass share important attacks; occasional
   fifth/octave movement connects the harmony. Keep low frequencies centred.
4. **Two guitar performances:** different registers, strum offsets and articulation;
   moderate left/right placement. Crunch should retain the pick attack.
5. **Human timing:** tiny bounded offsets, quieter upstrokes and ghost snares;
   fills announce section changes. No unbounded random playback.
6. **Website mix:** less foreground lead than a standalone song, restrained room
   ambience, level headroom, no metronome, seamless repeat, immediate sound off,
   silence under project films. The hologram's effects remain separate.

The MIDI is an editable compositional sketch. GarageBand is the instrument and
mixing environment; save its .band project and export the final audio from it.
`compose-workshop-rock.py` recreates the sketch only, not later DAW edits.

## User feedback and final direction

The initial MIDI version was heard by Lorenzo: “Not bad”, then “needs more
instruments” and “too synthetic”. That feedback superseded the MIDI rendering
approach. The final GarageBand session uses recorded guitar, bass, piano, organ
and tambourine parts with its adaptive Rock Drummer. Its 32-bar arrangement,
material names and mixing levels are documented in `README.md`. The MIDI sketch
remains an editable earlier idea, not the source of the shipped soundtrack.
